What's on the display

Everything on the display is real or left blank. The frequency is the published one for that feed. Scanner feeds that cover several channels show dashes and MULTI. The level bars and waveform come from the live audio. The weather is current conditions at the station from Open-Meteo. The green light on top comes on when a transmission is detected.

The AF flag means this browser is allowed to analyse the stream. LiveATC and the weather-radio servers allow it. Broadcastify usually doesn't, so those channels play normally but without level bars, waveform, transmission log or the narrowband filter.

How scan works

Scan picks the channel that is usually busiest at this moment, from its local time. Airports peak in morning and evening banks. Dispatch peaks in the evening. Rail follows commuter hours. Gander follows the North Atlantic track system: westbound flow is roughly midday to evening UTC, eastbound overnight. These are typical daily patterns, not live traffic counts.

Once on a channel, scan stays for at least two minutes. It moves on after 75 seconds of silence, and after twelve minutes regardless. Channels you've heard talking recently score higher. Weather radio is left out of the ALL scan because it never goes quiet. Pick WX to scan it.

Dual watch

Press DW to keep the current channel as a quieter priority channel while the main receiver moves on. When the priority channel talks, the main channel dips so you can hear it.

Volunteer feeds go off air without notice. If open-channel-status.json sits next to this page (written by the included GitHub Action), channels it found off air are marked red and skipped by scan. Otherwise each channel is marked when you tune it.

Sources

Air traffic and oceanic audio: LiveATC.net. Marine, rail and public-safety audio: Broadcastify (free listeners may hear a short ad when a feed starts). Weather radio: WXRadio and noaaweatherradio.org. Weather: Open-Meteo. Every stream plays straight from its provider and nothing is recorded or relayed. Each feed is run by volunteers who own it, and links on the station rows go to their pages.

Not for operational use. Audio can be delayed, interrupted or missing.

Open Channel streams public audio directly from the providers credited under Notes. If you run one of these feeds and want it removed, open an issue on the repository and it will come out.
